# 🌍 Research Background & Motivation

Urban transportation systems have experienced significant growth over the last decade due to increasing urbanization, population density, and the widespread adoption of ride-hailing platforms. Every day, millions of taxi drivers make route decisions that directly influence passenger waiting time, fuel consumption, operational efficiency, and overall transportation costs.

Traditional navigation systems are primarily designed to identify the shortest or fastest route to a known destination. However, taxi drivers often face a different challenge—they must decide **where to drive next when they do not yet have a passenger**. Choosing an unprofitable route can lead to long idle periods, increased fuel costs, and reduced daily earnings.

Consequently, intelligent taxi route recommendation has become an active research area within Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS), where machine learning techniques are used to identify regions with a high probability of passenger demand while considering both historical travel patterns and temporal traffic dynamics.

---

# 🚖 The Taxi Route Recommendation Problem

A taxi driver's profitability depends not only on transporting passengers efficiently but also on minimizing the time spent searching for the next passenger.

Several real-world challenges make this problem difficult:

- Highly dynamic traffic conditions
- Rapidly changing passenger demand
- Spatial relationships between neighboring regions
- Temporal variations throughout the day
- Peak and off-peak traffic patterns
- Weather and special-event influences
- Large-scale road network complexity

An effective recommendation system must therefore learn **both spatial and temporal patterns** from historical transportation data rather than relying solely on static shortest-path algorithms.

---

# 🧠 Why Graph Convolutional Networks (GCN)?

Transportation networks naturally form graph structures.

In a road network:

- Intersections or geographic regions can be represented as nodes.
- Road connections between regions become edges.
- Traffic flow and regional interactions form complex spatial relationships.

Traditional neural networks treat observations as independent samples and cannot effectively capture these graph-based dependencies.

Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) address this limitation by learning feature representations directly from graph-structured data. This enables the model to understand how neighboring regions influence one another and to capture spatial correlations that are essential for route recommendation.

Within this project, the GCN component is responsible for learning spatial representations of the transportation network.

---

# ⏳ Why Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM)?

Traffic conditions and passenger demand continuously evolve over time.

Patterns observed during:

- Morning rush hours
- Afternoon traffic
- Evening commuting periods
- Weekends
- Public holidays

are fundamentally different from one another.

Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks are specifically designed to model sequential and temporal dependencies. Their gated architecture enables them to retain important historical information while mitigating the vanishing gradient problem encountered by traditional recurrent neural networks.

In this project, the LSTM component learns temporal traffic behavior and demand trends across different time intervals.

---

# 🔗 Why Combine GCN and LSTM?

Neither spatial learning nor temporal learning alone is sufficient for accurate taxi route recommendation.

The hybrid GCN-LSTM architecture combines the strengths of both approaches:

- **GCN** captures spatial dependencies across the transportation network.
- **LSTM** models temporal changes in traffic and passenger demand.
- **Feature fusion** integrates both representations into a unified prediction model.

This combination enables the system to recommend routes based on both **where demand is likely to occur** and **when it is most likely to occur**, resulting in more informed and context-aware recommendations.

# 🌐 Graph Construction

A key contribution of the reproduced model is the representation of the transportation network as a graph.

The graph consists of:

- **Nodes** representing geographic regions or locations.
- **Edges** representing spatial relationships between neighboring regions.
- **Node features** describing traffic and demand characteristics.
- **Adjacency matrix** capturing graph connectivity.

This graph representation enables the Graph Convolutional Network to learn spatial dependencies that traditional neural networks cannot effectively model.

---

# ⏳ Temporal Sequence Generation

Passenger demand changes throughout the day, requiring the model to analyze sequential observations rather than isolated records.

Temporal preprocessing includes:

- Chronological ordering of observations
- Sliding-window sequence generation
- Daily sequence construction
- Hourly sequence construction
- Historical context preservation

These sequences become inputs to the LSTM component, allowing the model to capture long-term temporal dependencies.
